Physical Model – Concept

One frame, four swappable drive modules, all sharing a rotating paper platen and a marked pen arm. Every dial has an engraved scale (mm / teeth / degrees) matching the sliders above, so a setting on the screen can be copied onto the machine.

M1 · Planetary core

Ring gear (R teeth) + planet gear (r teeth) with a pen arm having numbered holes (offset d). Gives Circle, Ellipse, Line (Cardan: r = R/2), Cardioid, Flower, Background pattern.

M2 · Rack roller

Same planet gear rolls on a straight rack; pen at radius d from centre → Cycloid (d=r), curtate/prolate variants.

M3 · Polar drive

Platen turns (angle θ) while the pen slides radially on a lead-screw at a ratio b mm/rad → Archimedean Spiral. A third axis feeds the paper/pen height to draw a Helix and Toroid projection.

M4 · Cartesian cam / XY

Two lead-screw axes (or cut profile cams) with the Arduino generating x=t, y=t²/4f → Parabola, x=a·cosh t, y=b·sinh t → Hyperbola.

Block diagram

Arduino/ESP32 ─┬─ Stepper A (platen θ) ──► rotating paper table (scale: 0–360°)

Manual mode: hand-crank + interchangeable gear rings (no electronics needed)

Helix and toroid are 3-D curves; the machine draws their projections (front view = sinusoid, top view = circle/epitrochoid) and the demo shows the full 3-D view.

Cross-sections and polygons

The cross-section is the outline of one turn: the plan view of a helix, the shape of each turn of a spiral, the tube profile of a toroid and the plan view of the spiralling helix (a coil whose radius grows as it rises). It is defined by a radius function ρ(φ) (circumradius or half-width = 1): circular ρ=1; elliptical aspect b/a; squarish and rectangularish use a superellipse |x|ⁿ+|y/b|ⁿ=1 (n=2 round … n=20 almost sharp); triangular and hexagonal are regular polygons. Regular polygons of 3–12 sides are drawn as straight edges between n vertices. On the machine these are drawn electronically by the polar drive (the gear core cannot make them). The demo shows the matching firmware command under the drawing.

Build Guide (summary)

Bill of materials

3× NEMA17 + A4988 drivers · Arduino Nano/ESP32 · 1 servo · 12 V 3 A supply · 2× T8 lead screw (200 mm) · linear rails · 3D-printed/laser-cut gears (module 1, 30–150 teeth) · MDF/acrylic base · 16×2 LCD + rotary encoder · engraved scales.

Steps

1) Print the gear set, 2) assemble platen + radial slide, 3) glue ruler/degree scales, 4) wire the electronics, 5) load firmware computing the same equations as index.html, 6) calibrate steps/mm, 7) trace and compare against the demo.

Exhibition tips

Run the demo on a laptop next to the machine; show the same preset on both. Keep a poster with the table above. Use different colour pens for layers.

Circuit Diagram

Set MS1–MS3 high on both A4988 boards (1/16 microstep), set current limit to ~0.8 A, tie driver RESET to SLEEP, and add a 100 µF capacitor across each VMOT/GND. Share a common ground between the 12 V and 5 V sides.
